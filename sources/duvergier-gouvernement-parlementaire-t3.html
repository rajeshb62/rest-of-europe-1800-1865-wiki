Duvergier de Hauranne — Histoire du gouvernement parlementaire en France, 1814-1848, Tome III

Ernest Duvergier de Hauranne (1798–1881) — Histoire du gouvernement parlementaire en France, 1814-1848, précédée d’une Introduction. — Tome troisième. — Paris: Michel Lévy Frères, libraires-éditeurs, rue Vivienne, 2 bis, 1859 (droits de reproduction et de traduction réservés). — 570 fichiers, 21,054 lignes (~1.18 Mo en brut nettoyé).

This third volume of Duvergier de Hauranne’s parliamentary history of France opens with an Avertissement (Preface) in which the author acknowledges the warm reception given to the first two volumes and explains the delay in publication. He benefited from new communications, notably the papers of Élie Decazes made available through M. Forgues, which shed light on the pivotal ministries in which Decazes was the most influential member. The preface closes with the author’s intention to cover the legislative sessions of 1816, 1817 and 1818 in the next volume.

The volume covers the period from the Chamber of Representatives of the Hundred Days through the dissolution of the Chambre introuvable by the Ordonnance of 5 September 1816 — a crucial phase in which the nature of the Restoration regime was determined.

Notes

Series: Histoire du gouvernement parlementaire en France, 1814-1848; this third volume covers June–September 1815 through September 1816 (from the Hundred Days dissolution through the dissolution of the Chambre introuvable). Avertissement: The preface explains that new materials — notably the papers of Élie Decazes — allowed Duvergier to treat the Decazes ministries with greater authority. He promises forthcoming treatment of the sessions of 1816, 1817 and 1818 in the next volume. Edition: First edition (Michel Lévy Frères, Paris, 1859). Unlike Tomes I and II which are second and first editions respectively, this volume is the original 1859 printing. Scope: The volume is the first of two covering the actual Restoration (Tome I covered the Introduction through 1789-1813 and the early Restoration 1814-1815). Tome III begins with the Hundred Days Parliament (Chambre des Représentants) and ends with Louis XVIII’s dissolution of the Ultra-Royalist Chamber in September 1816 — the decisive moment when the king chose the moderate/constitutional path over Ultra reaction.

Placement: Rest of Europe 1800-1865 wiki. Chronologically the volume covers 1815-1816, entirely within the early Restoration period. Corpus: This volume sits alongside Delord (Histoire du second empire) and the other Duvergier volumes as French constitutional history sources. Duvergier writes as an Orléanist liberal — a partisan of parliamentary government in the English mode, hostile to both Ultra-Royalist reaction and Bonapartist authoritarianism.
