Friedjung — Österreich von 1848 bis 1860, Vol. II: Die deutsche Politik des Fürsten Schwarzenberg und die Reaktion 1849–1860

Heinrich Friedjung (1851–1920) — Österreich von 1848 bis 1860. Zweiter Band, Erste Abteilung. — Stuttgart and Berlin: J. G. Cotta’sche Buchhandlung, 3rd edition, 1912 (first published 1908).

The second volume of Friedjung’s narrative of Austria from 1848 to 1860, covering the years from the spring of 1849 to the middle of the 1850s. (The volume is labelled Erste Abteilung — the First Division — of the second volume; a further section carried the story into the later 1850s.) The narrative opens with the German policy of Prince Felix Schwarzenberg — the rejection of the Prussian attempt at a federal state, the rise and fall of the Erfurt Union, the Four-Kings’ alliance, the two meetings at Warsaw, the collision over Holstein and Hesse-Cassel, and the Punktation of Olmütz (1850) — and proceeds to Schwarzenberg’s death, the reaction under Kübeck and Bach, the Crimean War, and then a broad series of studies of the monarchy in the 1850s: the emperor and the court, the social structure, intellectual life, the theatre, the visual arts and the Vienna city expansion, and the church question down to the fall of Josephinism and the Concordat of 1855.

The volume is approximately 570 pages of text, extracted from a Michigan State University scan (Anna’s Archive), 598 scanned pages. A full table of contents (Inhalt) opens the volume, and it closes with three appendices of documents: letters of Prince Felix Schwarzenberg (Nos. 1–28), acta of 1850–1852, and a note on an alleged saying of Schwarzenberg, “Avilir puis démolir” (to debase and then to demolish).

Content Summary

Einleitung — Introduction

The opening of the second volume, carrying the reader from the position reached at the close of Vol. I.

Erstes Buch — Die deutsche Politik des Fürsten Schwarzenberg (April 1849 bis Mai 1850) (The German Policy of Prince Schwarzenberg, April 1849 to May 1850)

I. Die deutsche Kaiserkrone — the German imperial crown; the offer of the crown to Emperor Francis of Austria in 1814 and its refusal; the decision of 1849. II. Der preußisch deutsche Bundesstaat — the Prussian note of 9 May 1849 proposing a federal state (the German Union); the Prussian-German constitution of 28 May; the defeat of the Austrian army in Hungary; Schwarzenberg’s rejection of the Prussian proposal. III. Radowitz und seine Gegner — Radowitz and his opponents. IV. Das großdeutsche Programm Österreichs — the Greater-German program of Austria; the capitulation of the Hungarian army; the mediating influence of the Tsar; the meeting of Francis Joseph and Frederick William IV at Teplitz (9 September); the Austro-Prussian treaty of 30 September on the provisional central authority; the elections for the Erfurt parliament; the “Seventy-Million-Reich.”.

V. Das Vier-Königsbündnis gegen Preußen — the alliance of the four kings against Prussia; Austrian protests of 26 and 28 November; Bruck’s memorandum of 30 September 1849 on an Austro-German customs union; the Four-Kings’ alliance of 27 February 1850. VI. Das Erfurter Parlament — Radowitz’s conversations with Kübeck and Schönhals; the adoption of the federal constitution by the Erfurt parliament. VII. Wiederherstellung der Frankfurter Bundesversammlung — the restoration of the Frankfort Federal Diet; the Austro-Saxon agreement; Austria’s invitation to the federal congress (26 April); the congress of princes at Berlin; the opening of the Frankfort congress on 10 May; Bruck’s memorandum of 30 May 1850; armaments and danger of war.

Zweites Buch — Die deutsche Politik des Fürsten Schwarzenberg (Fortsetzung) (The German Policy of Prince Schwarzenberg, continued)

I. Erste Zusammenkunft in Warschau — the first meeting at Warsaw; the Tsar’s mediating attitude and Schwarzenberg’s dissatisfaction. II. Letzte Ausgleichsversuche — last attempts at compromise; Austria demanding the abolition of the Union constitution; Schwarzenberg’s offer to Bernstorff (8 July); the six points. III. Zusammenstoß wegen Holsteins und Kurhessens — the collision over Holstein and Hesse-Cassel; the battle of Idstedt; the London Protocol of 4 July; the constitutional dispute in Hesse-Cassel; the meeting and treaty of Bregenz. IV. Zweite Zusammenkunft in Warschau. Entlassung des Generals Radowitz — the second meeting at Warsaw and the dismissal of Radowitz; Brandenburg at Warsaw (17 October); the entry of the federal corps into Hesse-Cassel; Brandenburg’s return to Berlin and the Prussian ministerial council of 2 November; Radowitz’s resignation.

V. Kriegsgefahr — the danger of war; the Prussian mobilization of 6 November; the collision at Bronzell; Schwarzenberg’s offer of a common reactionary policy; Austria’s ultimatum; the proposal of a meeting at Olmütz. VI. Die Rüstungen Österreichs — Austria’s armaments; the organization of the Austrian army; the misplaced deployment of the Prussian forces; Heß’s war plan; Radetzky and the Austrian generals; the attitude of Russia, England, and France. VII. Olmütz — the meeting at Olmütz, 28 and 29 November 1850; the agreements (Punktation); the attitude of Francis Joseph; Schwarzenberg’s motives for yielding. VIII. Die Dresdener Konferenz — the Dresden Conference; the opening of the conferences on 24 December; Prussia’s insuperable resistance; the secret alliance between Austria and Prussia of 16 May 1851; the results.

Drittes Buch — Schwarzenberg auf der Höhe des Erfolges. Sein Tod. (Schwarzenberg at the Height of His Success. His Death.)

I. Österreich und die Großmächte — Austria and the great powers; Palmerston’s interference in Austrian affairs; the peaceful attitude of Queen Victoria; Palmerston and the Hungarian emigrants; Haynau in London; Palmerston’s approval of the French coup d’état and his dismissal; Schwarzenberg for Prince Napoleon; Austria and France against England. II. Österreichs Übergewicht in Deutschland — Austria’s predominance in Germany; the Federal Diet’s steps against the liberal German constitutions; Bernstorff, Manteuffel, and Vitzthum on Austria; Heinrich and Max von Gagern. III. Schwarzenberg, Metternich und Kübeck — a comparison of the politics of Metternich and Schwarzenberg; Metternich’s return to Vienna; Metternich and Kübeck against Schwarzenberg; Duke Ernst of Coburg in Vienna. IV. Schwarzenbergs Tod. Sein Charakter — Schwarzenberg’s illness and death (5 April 1852); Grillparzer’s epitaph; Laube, Metternich, and Wessenberg on Schwarzenberg; a comparison of Schwarzenberg with Bismarck.

Viertes Buch — Die Erbschaft nach dem Fürsten Schwarzenberg. Reaktion (1852–1854) (The Inheritance after Prince Schwarzenberg. Reaction, 1852–1854)

I. Kübeck und Bach — the emperor’s wish to make Bach minister-president; the resistance of Kübeck, Metternich, and Grünne; the post left unfilled; the creation of the Supreme Police Authority; the ministerial conference of 14 April 1852; the Tsar in Vienna (May 1852); Kübeck as the leading figure; Bach’s suppleness; Bach throwing himself into the arms of the Church. II. Innere Verwaltung — internal administration; the organization of 1853; the bureaucracy; the curtailment of communal autonomy. III. Justizwesen — the judiciary; Justice Minister Karl Krauß; the abolition of trial by jury; the criminal code of 1852; corporal punishment. IV. Polizeiherrschaft — police rule; Kempen as police minister; denunciations; the suppression of the press; conspiracies; the attempted assassination of the emperor by Libényi (18 February 1853).

V. Aufhebung des Belagerungszustandes — the lifting of the state of siege; the amnesties; Bach rising again; Kübeck’s death.

Fünftes Buch — Die Zeit des Krimkrieges (The Time of the Crimean War)

I. Buols Politik gegenüber Frankreich — the career of Count Buol; the erection of the French Empire; Francis Joseph in Berlin (January 1852); Bismarck on Buol. II. Die österreichische Herrschaft über Italien — Austrian rule over Italy; fines in Lombardy; Mazzini; the conspiracy and executions at Mantua; the Milan rising of 6 February 1853; Radetzky’s administration; Count Rechberg at Milan; the breach between Austria and Piedmont. III. Der montenegrinische Streitfall — the Montenegrin dispute; the war between Turkey and Montenegro; Austria’s intervention and ultimatum. IV. Der Krimkrieg — the Crimean War; Russia’s claim to the protectorate over the Christians of the Ottoman Empire; the Russian army crossing the Danube; Hübner and Prokesch-Osten against Russia; Bruck’s view; Austria’s alliance with Prussia of 20 April 1854; Austria demanding the evacuation of the Danubian Principalities; the end of the Austro-Russian alliance; Buol and Bach against Russia; mobilization and demobilization; Bruck made finance minister; the Congress of Paris; Austria evacuating the Principalities; Bismarck on Buol’s policy.

Sechstes Buch — Der Kaiser, der Hof, die soziale Schichtung (The Emperor, the Court, the Social Structure)

I. Kaiser Franz Joseph — Bismarck, Leopold I of Belgium, Gerlach, and Kübeck on Francis Joseph; his outward bearing; his knowledge of state affairs; his changes of mind. II. Die kaiserliche Familie — the imperial family; the emperor’s father; Archduchess Sophie; the Libényi attempt; Empress Elisabeth; the archdukes Ferdinand Max, Johann, and Stephan. III. Grünne und Heß — the adjutant-general Count Grünne; Heß as chief of the general staff; Empress Elisabeth and Grünne. IV. Die Aristokratie — the aristocracy; the legal position of the nobility; Prince Alfred Windischgrätz; Prince Friedrich Schwarzenberg, the “Landsknecht” (“lansquenet” / mercenary); historical right; Baron Andrian; the Estates opposition in Styria and Tyrol; a comparison of Austrian absolutism with the rule of Napoleon I.

V. Der dritte Stand — the third estate; the landholding peasant; the propertied bourgeoisie; the Jews; large industry and high finance; the House of Rothschild; social life in Vienna; the salons; the coffee-houses and clubs; social development and the nationalities.

Siebentes Buch — Geistiges Leben in Deutsch-Österreich (Intellectual Life in German Austria)

I. Grundlagen (1815–1848) — the wealth and limits of Austrian culture; intellectual pressure; political poetry; the end of Old Vienna and the beginning of the Vormärz; the general disappointment after the revolution. II. Musik — music; the Vienna Court Opera; Lanner and Strauß; the second half of the century. III. Lyrik — lyric poetry. IV. Erzählende Dichtung — narrative fiction; Sealsfield; Kürnberger; the time-novels; Stifter.

V. Politische Dichtung — political poetry; Grillparzer; Zedlitz, Marsano, Hartmann; Bauernfeld, Beck. VI. Presse — the press; the newspapers before 1848; Bäuerle and Saphir; Jáng; Kuranda; Warrens; Schwarzer, Schuselka, Friedmann; the feuilleton; the Allgemeine Zeitung.

Achtes Buch — Geistiges Leben in Deutsch-Österreich (Das Burgtheater und die dramatische Dichtung) (Intellectual Life in German Austria: The Burgtheater and Dramatic Poetry)

I. Das Burgtheater — the Burgtheater; Joseph II as its refounder; Schreyvogel as director; Laube as director. II. Die österreichischen Dramatiker — the Austrian dramatists; Friedrich Halm; Bauernfeld. III. Friedrich Hebbel in Wien — Friedrich Hebbel in Vienna; his political views; Gyges und sein Ring; the Nibelungen trilogy. IV. Grillparzers letzte Schöpfungen — Grillparzer’s last works; his autobiography; his testament; Libussa; Ein Bruderzwist in Habsburg; Die Jüdin von Toledo; the significance of the theatre for Austria.

Neuntes Buch — Geistiges Leben in Deutsch-Österreich (Das Volksstück) (Intellectual Life in German Austria: The Popular Play)

The three popular theatres in Vienna; the fairy play; Director Carl; Director Franz Pokorny; Friedrich Kaiser; Elmar; Berla, Langer; O. F. Berg; Johann Nestroy; Die Freiheit in Krähwinkel; Nestroy and the March revolution; the character of the Viennese popular play.

Zehntes Buch — Die bildenden Künste. Wiener Stadterweiterung (The Visual Arts. The Vienna City Expansion)

I. Die Zeit vor 1848 — art in the eighteenth century; monumental art under Emperor Francis and at Prague; Nobile; Sprenger; the Romantic; Peter Krafft; Danhauser; Waldmüller; the character of the Old-Vienna school of painting; Moritz von Schwind; Steinle. II. Die bildenden Künste von 1848 bis 1865 — the visual arts from 1848 to 1865; the Altlerchenfeld church in Vienna; Leo and Franz Thun; Ruben; Waldmüller’s dismissal; Friedrich Schmidt; Ferstel; Führich and his school; Van der Nüll and Siccardsburg; sculpture; Fernkorn; Hans Gasser; Pettenkofen, Leopold Müller; Rahl. III. Die Wiener Stadterweiterung — the Vienna city expansion; the imperial rescript of 20 December 1857; the first plan of regulation; military considerations; the court museums; the building over of the parade ground.

Elftes Buch — Kirchliches Leben bis zur Aufhebung der Gesetze Josephs II. 1850 (Ecclesiastical Life down to the Abrogation of the Laws of Joseph II, 1850)

I. Die Kirchenpolitik Josephs II. — the church policy of Joseph II; the so-called revocation of the Josephine legislation; Leopold II. II. Die Kirche unter Kaiser Franz und Kaiser Ferdinand — the Church under Emperors Francis and Ferdinand; the alliance of the state power with Rome; Staatsrat Martin Lorenz, Burgpfarrer Jakob Frint; Staatsrat Alois Jüstel; the canon-law teacher Dolliner. III. Bernard Bolzano — Friedrich Hurter as imperial historiographer; Bolzano’s philosophy; the persecution of Bolzano; his relations with Anna Hoffmann. IV. Die Romantik. Klemens Maria Hoffbauer — the Romantic movement in Vienna; the life of Clemens Maria Hoffbauer; Hoffbauer in Warsaw; his personality.

V. Antonio Rosmini. Anton Günther — liberal Catholicism; the life of Rosmini; the spread of Günther’s philosophy; orthodoxy against Günther. VI. Joh. Emanuel Veith. Erzbischof Milde — Veith’s youth and conversion; Veith as cathedral preacher at St. Stephen’s; liberal Catholicism; Archbishop Milde; Veith and the revolution of 1848. VII. Die Bischofsversammlung von 1849. Kardinal Schwarzenberg. Rauscher — the bishops’ assembly of 1849; the demand for the freedom of the Catholic Church; the constitution of 4 March 1849; Cardinal Friedrich Schwarzenberg; Joseph Othmar Rauscher and his opinions for the state chancellor; the bishops’ assembly at Vienna. VIII. Graf Leo Thun. Der Sieg der Bischöfe — Count Leo Thun; the union of the cults and instruction ministries; the attitude of Pius IX during the revolution; the opinions of Helfert and Ignaz Beidtel; the attitude of Schmerling and Bach; the emperor’s decision.

IX. Der Fall des Josephinismus — the fall of Josephinism; the imperial ordinances of 18 and 23 April 1850: the abrogation of the placetum regium, the restoration of ecclesiastical jurisdiction, the order of divine service, theological and religious instruction; the Concordat of 18 August 1855; the rule and freedom of the Church.

Rückblick — Retrospect

Friedjung’s closing retrospective on the period.

Anhang — Appendices

Anhang I — Letters of Prince Felix Schwarzenberg (Nos. 1–28). Anhang II — Acta 1850–1852 (Nos. 29–41), including No. 42 on the Vienna city expansion. Anhang III — A note on an alleged saying of Prince Felix Schwarzenberg, “Avilir puis démolir”.
