Delord — Histoire illustrée du second empire, Tome III

Taxile Delord (1815–1877) — Histoire illustrée du second empire. — Tome troisième. — Nouvelle édition. — Paris: Librairie Germer Baillière et Cie, 108, boulevard Saint-Germain, 1880 (printer: Coulommiers, Typographie Paul Brodard). 72 gravures dans le texte; 17 têtes de chapitre ou culs-de-lampe.

Republican journalist and deputy (Siècle), Delord writes a contemporary opposition history of the Second Empire as a continuous narrative, not memoir. This is the illustrated edition (Baillière [1880–83]) of the same multi-tome work originally published 1869–75. The present volume opens with the legislature of 1857–1863 (session of 1858, Orsini aftermath, law of sûreté générale) and carries the narrative through the Italian war’s later phases, the China and Syria expeditions, the Mexican expedition, the great elections of 1863, and into early 1864 (Carnot and Garnier-Pagès elected in Paris, foundation of the International, the 15 September Convention with Italy).

Core chronological span is 1857–1864 — the later authoritarian Empire, the liberal turn of the 1860 decree, the Italian unification crisis (Castelfidardo, Garibaldi’s conquest of the Two Sicilies), the Polish insurrection of 1863, the Danish question and the rise of Bismarck, and the beginnings of the Mexican adventure. The volume closes with the elections of 1863–64 and the first steps toward the liberal Empire.

Chapters

Chapitre I — Législature de 1857 à 1863

Sessions of 1858–1863: verification of powers, Migeon affair, law of sûreté générale, law on titles of nobility, the Paris works, the budget commission and the Conseil d’État. Session of 1859: the 500-million loan, clericals demand guarantees, Jules Favre speaks in the Corps législatif. Brame’s proposition, the Grand Central, annexation of the banlieue. Session of 1860: contested elections, official candidacy, the commercial treaty, financial speciality, loans to industry. Session of 1861: imperial discourse to Senate and Corps législatif, Troplong’s report on the senatus-consult, Prince Napoléon’s speech, the cardinals’ amendment rejected, Dupin and the press, stamp on the roman-feuilleton. Session of 1862: address discussed in Senate (religious question, Persigny called Polignac, Rome), La Guéronnière and Prince Napoléon, Mexico. Corps législatif: thirty-year obligations, the Palikao endowment, the commercial-free-trade inquiry, income-tax proposal by Granier de Cassagnac and Roques-Salvaza. Opening of the 1863 session: Emperor’s retrospective review since 1857, Thouvenel’s speech, Billault, Darimon petition, Ollivier’s discourse, Baroche’s reply, Mexico debate (Jules Favre, Jérôme David), modification of the Penal Code.

Chapitre II — Suite de la Guerre d’Italie

January 1860 reception, the brochure Le Pape et le Congrès, the 5 January programme, the commercial treaty with England (Michel Chevalier and Richard Cobden), cession of Nice and Savoy to France, Thouvenel’s dispatch, Napoleon III’s letter to Persigny. Weakness of the dictatorial Empire.

Chapitre III — Expéditions de Chine et de Syrie

China expedition: causes, Treaty of Tien-tsin, delay in ratification, Anglo-French expedition (Lord Elgin and Baron Gros), taking of the Pei-ho forts, Pa-li-kao battle, flight of the Emperor of China to Manchuria, capture and pillage of the Summer Palace, destruction of the Summer Palace, Prince Hong-kong and General Ignatieff bring the Chinese government to sign peace, return of the army, preparations for the Cochinchina expedition. Syria expedition: the Lebanon, state of the Orient since the Sepoy revolt and the Crimean War, the massacres of the Lebanon and of Damascus, news reaches France, Napoleon III prepares to succour the Christians of Syria, diplomatic preliminaries, resistance of Turkey, difficulties raised by England and Russia, Piedmont excluded from negotiations, Fuad-Pasha at Beirut and Damascus, slowness of repression, indemnity, result of the expedition at the end of 1860.*

Chapitre IV — Castelfidardo

Impotence of the Villafranca convention. Imperial policy shifts in a sense favourable to Italy; the brochure Le Pape et le Congrès; difficulties raised by the proposal for a congress; the four propositions. Annexation of Savoy and Nice to France. Expedition to Sicily. Chambéry interview. Preparations for war; Piedmontese troops enter Pontifical territory. Battle of Castelfidardo. The kingdom of Naples and its interior situation. Attempts at alliance between Naples and Piedmont. Garibaldi crosses the strait. Conquest of the Two Sicilies by Garibaldi. Victor-Emmanuel enters Naples. Francis II at Gaeta, his capitulation and retreat to Rome.

Chapitre V — 1860–1861

The decree of 24 November; its causes. 1 January reception at the Tuileries. Application of the decentralization decree to the city of Paris. Rumours of dissolution of the Corps législatif. Rivalry of Persigny and Haussmann. Reception of Father Lacordaire at the French Academy; the Empress attends. Arrest of financier Mirès; Delangle’s report. The duc d’Aumale’s brochure: Lettre sur l’histoire de France. Seizure of the duc de Broglie’s Vues sur le gouvernement de la France. The Blanqui trial. Inauguration of the boulevard Malesherbes. The 25 millions for chemins vicinaux. Transformation of Paris. Industrial and financial crisis. The thirty-year obligations. Fould’s memoir addressed to the Emperor. Stockbrokers seek authorization for a statue of the Emperor. The Plassiart trial.

Chapitre VI — La Question des Duchés

Illness of the king of Prussia; the heir presumptive assumes the regency. Napoleon III and the regent of Prussia at Baden. Bismarck named Prussian ambassador at St Petersburg. Accession of Wilhelm I. Army reform. Awakening of unitary spirit. The Backer attentat and its influence on the king. Coronation of the king of Prussia. The principle of nationalities and the question of the Duchies. Effect of the February Revolution on Germany. The campaign of the free corps. London conference. The Danish question subsides but the regent of Prussia revives it in 1859. Denmark counts on France and England. State of Poland. Troubles at Warsaw. Address to the Emperor. Dissolution of the agricultural society. Death of Prince Adam Czartoriski. England, Austria, Prussia, France and the insurrection; discourse of Prince Napoléon.

Chapitre VII — Mort de Cavour

Rome declared capital of Italy. Cavour’s illness and death. Italy recognized by Napoleon III. Ricasoli succeeds Cavour. United States: the slavery question, election of Lincoln, the War of Secession. End of the Syria expedition and its results.

Chapitre VIII — L’Année 1862

1 January reception at the Tuileries. Fould’s financial programme. Trials and arrests. Société du Prince impérial. Imperial letter on the Roman question. Imperial voyage through central France. Morny created duke. Reversal of imperial policy on the Roman question: Thouvenel resigns, Drouyn de Lhuys replaces him. Napoleon III and mediation in the United States. Prohibition of the rue de la Paix conferences. Polemic over Le Fils de Giboyer. Inauguration of the boulevard du Prince-Eugène. Budberg, Russian ambassador, presents credentials. Imperial discourse. Subscription for cotton workers. Revival of the title of chevalier.

Chapitre IX — La Presse et le Clergé (1860–1862)

Expulsion of Ganesco (editor of the Courrier du dimanche). Difficulty over the reporting of judicial debates. Obscurity of the senatus-consult. Leymarie’s attempts to buy the Courrier de Paris; Billault and d’Haussonville. Hippolyte Castille as editor-in-chief; replaced by Clément Duvernois. Persigny and the press. Masson’s brochure finds no printer. Les Muses d’État. Laprade removed. Millaud buys La Presse; mass resignation of editors. The clergy and King Jérôme. Mandements and funeral ceremonies. La France, Rome et l’Italie by La Guéronnière. Bishop of Poitiers deféré as d’abus to the Conseil d’État. Religious proselytism; Lille trial. The Société de Saint-Vincent de Paul: the government and the society; Persigny’s circular; Rouland’s circular. Suppression of religious houses. Renan named to the Hebrew chair at the Collège de France; his destitution. The jubilee of Toulouse.

Chapitre X — Expédition du Mexique (1858–1861)

Mexico under Spanish domination; the war of independence; Iturbide’s empire and its fall. Effect of pronunciamentos. Formation of the liberal party; Mexico and Santa Anna; triumph of the liberal party; Juarez presidency. Struggle between the liberal party and the clerical party. The Jecker loan. The Mexican republic and the European powers. The monarchical Mexican party abroad; it surrounds the Emperor and Empress; Morny’s support. The Jecker bonds. Dubois de Saligny replaces Gabriac; he begins with the Jecker claim. Juarez forced to suspend customs payments. Spain, England, and France. The United States.

Chapitre XI — Les Français au Mexique

Diplomatic negotiations; the London convention. The Spanish arrive before the English and French at Mexico; they occupy Vera-Cruz. Preliminaries of La Soledad. Manifesto of the allied commissioners; their rupture. Conference of Orizaba; the conference separates. Denunciation of the rupture. Attack on Orizaba by General Lorencez. Pronunciamento of the city in favour of Almonte. March on Puebla; French army repulsed. Period Lorencez; reinforcements sent. General Forey replaces Lorencez. Public opinion and Mexico at the end of 1862.

Chapitre XII — Les Élections dans les Départements

Early 1863; the cotton crisis. Distribution of rewards to French exhibitors at London. Prohibition of public readings for the benefit of workers of the Seine-Inférieure. Seizure of the Histoire de la maison de Condé. Moniteur note on parliamentary reporting. Academy election: Bishop Dupanloup and Littré. The 1863 elections in the departments. Garnier-Pagès’s tour. State of opinion in the great cities. Thiers’s candidacy; the oath question. The Union libérale. The Catholic party. Legitimism and Orleanism. Official candidacy. Result of the departmental elections.

Chapitre XIII — Les Élections à Paris

Republican party in Paris; its divisions; abstention; the party of the Siècle; the party of 1848; the party of new men; the party of the Cinq. Rivalries between candidates in the 5th circonscription; Havin and Ernest Picard. The central committee; Carnot’s meeting; failure to found a central committee. Government opposition to meetings. Moniteur note. Thiers’s candidacy; the Siècle does not accept it. First opposition list: Havin, Laboulaye, Ollivier, Ernest Picard, Jules Favre, Guéroult, Darimon, Jules Simon, Pelletan. Laboulaye withdraws in favour of Thiers. The Siècle decides to support Thiers. Persigny’s maladroitness in combating the candidacy; his circular to prefects; his letter to the prefect of the Seine. Paris elects the entire opposition list. Effect of the 31 May vote on Europe. Persigny forced to resign. Ministerial change. Billault minister of state; his death; Rouher replaces him. Empress’s voyage to Spain. Napoleon III proposes a congress to the powers; 300-million loan; congress aborted. End of 1863.

Chapitre XIV — Le Nouveau Cabinet

Persigny forced to resign. A new ministry forms: Billault, Boudet, Duruy, Behic, Rouher. Morny’s letter. Billault’s death; difficulty finding his successor; two possible men: Rouher and Baroche. Rouher: his beginnings, Minister of the President under the coup d’état, resignation as garde des sceaux over the 22 January decrees, president of section at the Conseil d’État, entry into the cabinet in 1855 as minister of agriculture, commerce and public works; replaces Billault at the ministry of state.

Chapitre XV — Napoléon III et l’Europe

Polish affairs. Grand Duke Constantine named viceroy of Poland. Attempts on Wielopolski and the Grand Duke. The Polish insurrection; repression by Mouraview; the cabinets of the Tuileries, London, and Vienna. Negotiations with Russia; Prince Gortschakoff ends them cavalierly. The princes’ day at Frankfurt. Influence on the Polish question and the Danish question. Denmark’s situation in 1862. The Gotha dispatch. Concessions of Christian VIII; Prussia and Austria reject them. Imprudence of passages in the Emperor’s opening speeches. Congress rendered impossible by England. Bismarck and England. The European concert and Denmark. Dismemberment of the Danish monarchy. Treaty of Vienna.

Chapitre XVI — Maximilien au Mexique

Siege of Puebla. Combat of San-Lorenzo. French entry into Mexico City. The triumvirate. Convocation of a constituent assembly. A deputation carries the offer of the crown to Maximilian. The council of regency. Establishment of martial courts; the contre-guérilla; military and administrative cruelties. Marshal Forey replaced by General Bazaine; Dubois de Saligny by Montholon. Arrival of the Mexican deputation at Miramar. Maximilian’s hesitations. Napoleon III’s impatience; he summons Maximilian and his wife to Paris; convention of 12 March. Further hesitations. Voyage of General Frossard to Miramar. The Archduchess Charlotte at Vienna. Treaty of Miramar; its three secret articles. Maximilian and Charlotte depart for Mexico; they stop at Rome. Arrival at Vera-Cruz; general indifference. Journey from Vera-Cruz to Mexico. Official reception. Installation of the imperial household.

Chapitre XVII — L’Année 1864

Beginning of 1864. Receptions at the Tuileries. Arrest of four Italians. Partial elections at Paris. Committee of electoral reform. Candidacies in the 1st circonscription (Pasquet, Carnot, Laboulaye, Pinard); in the 5th circonscription (Garnier-Pagès, Bancel, Bac, Renan, Vautrain, Hugelmann, Morin, Alix, Jules Ferry). Tolain, worker’s candidate. Carnot and Garnier-Pagès elected. Prohibition of public readings for the benefit of the Poles. Prohibition of the banquet in honour of Shakespeare. Dufaure received at the French Academy. Trial of the Thirteen. Foundation of the International Workingmen’s Association. Convention of 15 September. Vuitry minister presiding the Conseil d’État. Persigny’s letter to Girardin; the government rebukes it. Encyclopaedia of 8 December. Voltaire’s heart at the Bibliothèque nationale. Prince Napoléon vice-president of the privy council.

Notes

Series: Histoire illustrée du second empire by Taxile Delord; this is the Tome troisième of the illustrated edition (Baillière [1880–83]), corresponding to the third volume of the original 1869–75 Baillière set (OCLC 6266334). Tome II of the original edition is in this wiki; Tome IV and Tome V sit in the 1866–1919 wiki. Placement: Rest of Europe 1800–1865 wiki — the volume covers 1857–1864, from the later authoritarian Empire through the liberal turn and the Italian, Chinese, Syrian, and Mexican expeditions. The later 1865–1867 period (Sadowa, end of the Mexican expedition) is covered by Tome IV and Tome V in the 1866–1919 wiki. Voice: Contemporary republican narrative history; represent what Delord states, without modern scholarly reframing. Illustrations: 72 wood engravings in the text plus 17 decorative headpieces and tailpieces (not preserved in this raw OCR).

Related in corpus: Ollivier L’Empire libéral tomes V and VII (participant memoir-history of later imperial years) sit under the same French Second Empire shelf in this wiki. Raw: delord-histoire-second-empire-t3.txt.
