La Farina — Storia d’Italia dal 1815 al 1850, Vol. V: Documenti, Parte 1.2

Giuseppe La Farina (1815–1863) — Storia d’Italia dal 1815 al 1850. Volume V: Documenti. Parte 1.2. — Torino: Società Editrice Italiana, 1851.

Second documentary volume, complementing Volume III. It is the fullest single gathering in this corpus of the constitutions and statutes granted across the peninsula in 1847–1849, preceded by a first series of Restoration and early-revolutionary papers. About 650 pages; Indice dei documenti near pp. 651–657 (printed variants 351–357). Letter codes run very far (into triple letters); OCR glues titles. For constitutional wording this volume is usually the first stop; for Vienna and 1831 diplomacy, cross-check Volume III.

Serie I — Documenti A–DD (roughly 1815–1831)

Serie I reproduces diplomatic and state papers of the Restoration and of the revolutions of 1820–1821 and 1831 in a lettered sequence A through DD. Content overlaps thematically with Volume III’s first series—Vienna settlement language, southern constitutions and concordats, congress interventions, Legations crisis—but the 1851 editorial cut and lettering are those of this volume. Use both document volumes when hunting a specific act; do not assume identical completeness. The series supplies the legal backdrop against which La Farina’s narrative of Carboneria, Laybach, and 1831 is written.

Serie II — Documenti A–ZZZ (1847–1849 constitutions and acts)

Serie II is the constitutional heart of the whole Storia. Following the printed index, representative pieces include:

A protest of Cardinal Ciacchi (6 August 1847);. B protest of the Legate of Ferrara;. C treaty Tuscany–Duke of Lucca (4 October 1847);.

D–E acts of Lucca’s abdication (5 October);. F Grand Duke of Tuscany’s proclamation (11 October);. G motu proprio creating the Tuscan Consulta di Stato (“Council of State”) in numbered titles;.

further Tuscan and papal acts H–J;. K sovereign act of the Two Sicilies (18 January 1848);. L administration of Sicily;.

M Neapolitan constitution (29 January 1848);. N Statuto delle Due Sicilie (10 February 1848) in its capi;. O Sicilian Parliament (24 February) with instructions;.

P–Q Piedmontese statute of 4 March 1848—the text later called Statuto albertino (“Albertine Statute”), the charter that survived when others fell and became the constitutional law of the Regno d’Italia (“Kingdom of Italy”);. R–S Tuscan Statute (15 February) in titoli;. T Statuto della Santa Chiesa (14 March) for the Papal State;.

U–V Tuscan administrative ordinances (March);. X Neapolitan electoral law (29 February) with district apparatus;. Z constitution of Monaco (12 February);.

AA Austria–Archduke of Modena treaty.

The alphabet continues through Lombard provisional government, Venice, union with Piedmont, and the Repubblica romana (“Roman Republic”), out to ZZZ. Armistice instruments and revolutionary organic laws sit among the later letters.

What the volume does for the reader is place the legal creativity of 1848 side by side: princely grants under street pressure, Sicilian parliamentary identity, Piedmont’s durable charter, Tuscan and papal statutes, and republican organic laws. La Farina’s selection argues silently that Italians already wrote modern public law for themselves before 1859—and that only one monarchical statute outlasted the restorations of 1849.

Reading with the narrative and with Tivaroni

Pair Serie II with Volumes II and IV for the political circumstances of each grant and repeal. Pair the Statuto albertino texts with Tivaroni’s L’Italia degli Italiani for how the charter actually functioned in the Piedmontese connubio (“union” — the Cavour–Rattazzi alliance) years. Pair Sicilian and Neapolitan statutes with La Farina’s own southern narrative and with Tivaroni’s volumes on the Two Sicilies under Austrian-era diplomacy and French-era memory. The document volumes are not annotated to modern critical standards; they are working books for a national public.

Nature of the volume

Vol. 5 is a documenti companion: proclamations, diplomatic notes, statutes, letters, and contemporary pieces La Farina appends so the narrative volumes can be checked. In this wiki it is not a second storyline but a primary-text quarry for quotations and institutional wording already framed in vols. 1–4.

How to use in compilation

  1. Identify the decree, statute, or letter type from the index/headings in the raw OCR.
  2. Pull wording for Statuto-era, 1848 war, or provisional government pages only when the document is actually present—do not invent archival citations.
  3. Prefer short contemporary phrases (with IT gloss) over paraphrase when the document is a public act.
  4. Cross-link the narrative volume page that the document illustrates rather than building freestanding entities from a single undated scrap.

Content clusters (typical of the documenti series)

War proclamations and armistice-related papers of 1848–49; constitutional and municipal acts of provisional regimes; pieces touching Piedmontese parliamentary life; materials on central and southern crises as La Farina selected them for a unitarian readership.
