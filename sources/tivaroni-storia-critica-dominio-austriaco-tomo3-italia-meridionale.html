Tivaroni — Storia critica del risorgimento italiano, Tomo III: L’Italia durante il dominio austriaco (1815–1849). L’Italia meridionale: Lo svolgimento del pensiero nazionale

Carlo Tivaroni (1843–1906) — Storia critica del risorgimento italiano. Tomo III: L’Italia durante il dominio austriaco (1815–1849). L’Italia meridionale: Lo svolgimento del pensiero nazionale. — Torino: L. Roux e C., 1894.

The final volume of the second series-within-the-work of Tivaroni’s critical history, treating the Austrian Restoration in southern Italy. Where the first two dominio austriaco volumes covered the north and the centre, this third completes the series by turning to the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies and then to the intellectual elaboration of the national idea. It carries the story from the Restoration of 1815 through the Neapolitan revolution of 1820–21, the reaction and the revolutions of 1848–49, to the defeat of the Bourbon Monarchy in the Sicilian upheaval of 1849.

Tivaroni treats the south in two historical Parti — the Kingdom of Naples and Sicily — and closes with a third, the synthetic survey of the development of national thought that gives the volume its subtitle. The treatment of the south follows the fortunes of the Bourbon dynasty from Ferdinand I through the reactionary reign of Ferdinand II, while the final Parte gathers the writers and statesmen of the movement into a single intellectual portrait of the age.

The volume is approximately 704 pages of text, extracted from a University of California scan (Anna’s Archive). It is the sequel to the two northern and central volumes of the dominio austriaco series, which cover the same span (1815–1849).

Content Summary

Parte VIII — Il Regno di Napoli

The Kingdom of Naples under the restored Bourbons, from the Restoration to the revolution of 1848.

Capitolo I — La ristorazione — the Restoration: the promises and the treaties, the machinery of the restored state, the brigands, and the Concordat. Capitolo II — Il 1820 — the revolution of 1820: the carbonari, the pronunciamento, the Murattians in government, the Spanish constitution, the parliament, and the rout at Rieti. Capitolo III — Napoli borbonizzata (1821–1844) — Naples under the pure Bourbon regime: the vengeance of King Ferdinand, King Francis and the rising of the Cilento, Ferdinand II and the hopes he raised, Ferdinand II and the pure Bourbon regime, and the Cosenza conspiracies and the expedition of the Bandiera brothers. Capitolo IV — Il 1848 — the revolution of 1848: from 1845 to 1847, the granting of the constitution, 15 May, and the revolt and the reaction.

Parte IX — La Sicilia

The fate of Sicily as a province of the Two Sicilies, in revolt against Bourbon rule.

Capitolo I — La Sicilia provincia — Sicily as a province: the King of the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies, and from 1816 to 1820. Capitolo II — La rivoluzione del 1820 — the revolution of 1820 and the Murattians in government. Capitolo III — Dal 1821 al 1846 — from 1821 to 1846: the Marchese Pietro Ugo delle Favare, the repression of General Del Carretto, and the condition of Sicily. Capitolo IV — La rivoluzione del 1848 — the revolution of 1848: the outbreak at Messina and Palermo, the parliament that demanded independence, the struggle between the Bourbons and Sicily, and the conclusion.

Parte X — Lo svolgimento del pensiero nazionale

A synthetic survey of the development of the national idea across Italy, the intellectual dimension that completes the narrative of the Austrian domination.

Lo svolgimento del pensiero nazionale — the development of national thought: the necessity of conspiracy, Austria in control, the growth of the sects, the Congress of Verona, exiles and combatants, the year 1831, Young Italy and the work of Giuseppe Mazzini, the elaboration of the idea, reformers and revolutionaries, the condition of Italy, 1848, unity and federation, the culture of the century, errors and virtues, the terrible year, and the portraits of the leading figures of the movement — Carlo Botta, Pietro Colletta, Silvio Pellico, Alessandro Manzoni, Giacomo Leopardi, Giuseppe Giusti, Giovanni Battista Niccolini, Giuseppe Montanelli, Francesco Domenico Guerrazzi, Carlo Cattaneo, Guglielmo Pepe, Nicolò Tommaseo, Daniele Manin, Terenzio Mamiani, Pellegrino Rossi, Gino Capponi, Cesare Balbo, Vincenzo Gioberti, Pio IX, Carlo Alberto, and Mazzini himself.

Note on the text: The OCR is Italian. The printed Indice appears near the close of the volume. This final Parte is less narrative than the two that precede it; it moves from the story of the southern revolution to the intellectual history of the whole national movement. Tivaroni’s treatment of the southern revolutionaries — the carbonari, the Bandiera brothers, and the Sicilian insurgents — is, as throughout the work, sympathetic to the national cause.

Extended chapter-use guide (Wave MEDIUM)

Tomo 3 is the southern panel of Austrian-age dominion history: Naples and Sicily under Bourbons inside the post-1815 security system Tivaroni criticises.

Bourbon restoration machinery

Police, special courts, and fiscal spine of the Two Sicilies after 1815; carbonari seasons and 1820 constitutional episode as treated in the tome’s chapter order.

1848 in the south

Sicilian and Neapolitan revolutionary calendars, grants and revocations of constitutions, and the military reconquest that makes 1849–60 reaction intelligible.
