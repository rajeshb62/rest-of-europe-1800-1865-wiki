# Treitschke — Deutsche Geschichte im neunzehnten Jahrhundert, Vol. I: Bis zum zweiten Pariser Frieden

**Heinrich von Treitschke (1834–1896)** — *Deutsche Geschichte im neunzehnten Jahrhundert. Erster Theil: Bis zum zweiten Pariser Frieden.* — Leipzig: Verlag von S. Hirzel, 1879.

The opening volume of Treitschke's monumental five-volume history of Germany in the nineteenth century, a work of Prussian-nationalist historiography that became a canonical text of German historical writing. This first volume is itself Volume 24 of the series *Staatengeschichte der neusten Zeit*.

The volume covers an enormous chronological sweep. The **Erstes Buch** (First Book) is an extended introduction tracing the long decline and final dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire, from the Peace of Westphalia (1648) through the rise of Prussia, the cultural flowering of German letters, the impact of the French Revolution and Napoleon, the collapse of the Empire in 1806, Prussia's humiliation and subsequent reform, the Wars of Liberation (1813–1815), and the final defeat of Napoleon. The **Zweites Buch** (Second Book) then treats the beginnings of the German Confederation, from the Congress of Vienna (1814–1815) through the Battle of Waterloo and the Second Peace of Paris (1815).

Treitschke's narrative is avowedly partisan: he writes as a Prussian patriot and a liberal nationalist, celebrating the forces he believes prepared the way for German unification under Prussian leadership. The preface acknowledges the influence of Heinrich von Sybel and makes clear the work's debt to the Berlin State Archives and the Baden Foreign Ministry archives.

The text is approximately 500 pages, extracted from Stanford University Libraries copy (OCR from Google Books).

## Content Summary

### Erstes Buch — Einleitung: Der Untergang des Reichs (Introduction: The Downfall of the Empire)

A sweeping survey of German history from the Peace of Westphalia to the end of the Napoleonic Wars, designed to explain the forces that destroyed the old Reich and created the conditions for German national unity.

- **1. Deutschland nach dem Westphälischen Frieden** — the condition of Germany after the Thirty Years' War; the territorial fragmentation; the legacy of the Peace of Westphalia in constitutional and religious terms
- **2. Die Reichsverfassung** — the constitutional structure of the Holy Roman Empire; the powers of the Emperor, the Electoral College, the Imperial Diet, the Imperial Circles, and the Imperial Courts
- **3. Der preußische Staat** — the rise of Brandenburg-Prussia from the Great Elector through Frederick the Great; the character of Prussian institutions, its army, its administrative tradition
- **4. Die neue Literatur** — the German literary renaissance from Lessing and Klopstock through Goethe and Schiller; the cultural preconditions for national awakening
- **5. Revolution und Fremdherrschaft** — the impact of the French Revolution on Germany; the spread of revolutionary ideas; the beginning of French domination
- **6. Der Revolutionskrieg bis zum Baseler Frieden** — the Revolutionary Wars from Valmy to the Peace of Basel (1795); Prussia's withdrawal from the coalition; the secularisation and territorial reorganisation of Germany
- **7. Friedrich Wilhelm III. Der Rastatter Kongreß** — the character and policies of Frederick William III; the Congress of Rastatt (1797–1799); the failure of the Imperial Deputation
- **8. Dichtung** — the state of German poetry and intellectual life at the turn of the century; Romanticism; the German philosophical movement
- **9. Auflösung des Reichs. Krieg von 1806** — the dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire (1806); the Confederation of the Rhine; the disastrous War of 1806; the Battle of Jena-Auerstedt; Napoleon's occupation of Berlin
- **10. Preußens Erhebung** — the reawakening of Prussia after the catastrophe of 1806; patriotic movements; the intellectual and moral regeneration of the Prussian state
- **11. Stein. Scharnhorst. Das neue Deutschthum** — the reforms of Baron vom Stein; Scharnhorst's military reforms and the creation of a new army; the emergence of a new German national consciousness
- **12. Ministerium Altenstein. Krieg von 1809** — the ministry of Altenstein; the abortive war of 1809 against Napoleon; Austria's defeat and the Treaty of Schönbrunn
- **13. Rheinbündische Zustände. Hardenbergs Verwaltung. Deutsche Kunst** — conditions in the Confederation of the Rhine states; Hardenberg's administrative reforms in Prussia; the continued development of German art and culture under French hegemony
- **14. Der Befreiungskrieg** — the War of Liberation (1813–1814)
  - *Die Vorbereitung* — the preparations for the rising against Napoleon; the Convention of Tauroggen; the call to the people
  - *Frühjahrsfeldzug. Waffenstillstand* — the Spring Campaign of 1813; the Battle of Lützen and Bautzen; the Armistice of Pläswitz
  - *Die Zeit der Siege* — the great victories of 1813: Kulm, Dennewitz, the Battle of the Nations at Leipzig; the collapse of French power in Germany
- **15. Ende der Kriegszeit. Befreiung des Westens. Kriegspläne** — the end of the war period; the liberation of western Germany; the Allied war plans for the invasion of France
- **16. Der Winterfeldzug** — the winter campaign of 1814 on French soil; the Battle of Paris; the abdication of Napoleon
- **17. Friede und Heimkehr** — the First Peace of Paris; the return of the Prussian and German soldiers; the mood of the nation at the end of the wars

### Zweites Buch — Die Anfänge des Deutschen Bundes 1814–1819 (The Beginnings of the German Confederation)

The establishment of the new German order after the fall of Napoleon.

- **1. Der Wiener Congreß** — the Congress of Vienna
  - *Charakter des Congresses. Die Personen* — the character of the Congress; the leading figures: Metternich, Alexander I, Hardenberg, Castlereagh, Talleyrand; the social life of the Congress
  - *Die Gebietsverhandlungen* — the territorial negotiations; the restructuring of Germany; the contest between Austria and Prussia
  - *Der Deutsche Bund* — the creation of the German Confederation; its constitution, institutions, and weaknesses
- **2. Belle Alliance** — the campaign of 1815
  - *Der Belgische Feldzug* — Napoleon's return from Elba; the Belgian campaign; the Battle of Waterloo (Belle Alliance)
  - *Der zweite Pariser Friede* — the Second Peace of Paris; the new European settlement; the final end of the Napoleonic era

The volume closes with a **Berichtigungen** (errata) page.
