## Scope

This volume covers the reign of Friedrich Wilhelm IV of Prussia from his accession in 1840 to the eve of the March 1848 Revolution. The Vorwort is dated Berlin, 10 August 1894. Treitschke describes the period as a tragedy: "ein erschütterndes Trauerspiel" — lofty hopes followed everywhere by a pitiful collapse.

The central theme is the deepening crisis of the German Confederation, the rising national and liberal opposition, the church conflicts, and the gathering storm of revolution.

## Key Themes

1. **Friedrich Wilhelm IV** — the romantic on the throne, his personality, his constitutional and political ideas, his conflicts with the liberal movement
2. **The Rhine crisis (1840)** and the European peace system — the Straits Convention, the Near Eastern Question
3. **Church and state conflicts** — the Cologne affair, Hermesianism, the Prussian union with Rome, Deutschkatholizismus, the first Protestant synod
4. **Economic transformation** — the Zollverein's expansion, the railway boom, the tariff debates, the first social tensions
5. **The Polish and Schleswig-Holstein questions** — the Galician uprising, the incorporation of Kraków, the "Open Letter" crisis
6. **The United Diet (1847)** — Prussia's first all-estates parliament, the prelude to revolution
7. **The disintegration of the German Confederation** — the collapse of monarchical authority in the middle states, Prussia's federal reform initiatives
8. **The European prelude to 1848** — the Swiss Sonderbund War, Austria's position, the coming revolution
