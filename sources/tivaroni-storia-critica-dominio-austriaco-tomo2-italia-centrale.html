Tivaroni — Storia critica del risorgimento italiano, Tomo II: L’Italia durante il dominio austriaco (1815–1849). L’Italia centrale

Carlo Tivaroni (1843–1906) — Storia critica del risorgimento italiano. Tomo II: L’Italia durante il dominio austriaco (1815–1849). L’Italia centrale. — Torino: L. Roux e C., 1893.

The second volume of the Austrian-domination series of Tivaroni’s critical history, carrying the same chronological span (1815–1849) into central Italy. It treats the Tuscan Grand Duchy and the Papal State through the years of the Restoration, the moderate liberal movement, the revolutions of 1831 and 1848, and the collapse of the Roman Republic in 1849. Where the first volume covered the north, the present volume follows the fortunes of the two great central states whose fate shaped the national question in the peninsula’s middle.

Tivaroni treats the centre in two Parti — Tuscany and the Papal State — and carries each through the full arc from the Restoration to 1849. The volume is a study of the moderate reformism of Tuscany against the clerical absolutism of Rome, and of how both were overtaken by the revolutionary crisis of 1848–1849.

The volume is approximately 472 pages of text, extracted from a University of California scan (Anna’s Archive).

Content Summary

Parte VI — La Toscana

The Grand Duchy of Tuscany from the Restoration to the revolution.

La Ristorazione — the Restoration of 1815: Ferdinand III and Vittorio Fossombroni. Leopoldo II — Venti anni di governo paterno — twenty years of paternal government under Leopold II: the Antologia and the moderate liberal intellectual life, the reforms, and the quiet reformism of the Tuscan grand dukes. La Rivoluzione — the revolution of 1848 in Tuscany: the first steps, the ministry of Ridolfi, the ministry of Capponi, the baraonda (turmoll) of Montanelli and Guerrazzi, and the restoration of the constitution (12 April). Lucca — the Duchy of Lucca and its annexation to Tuscany.

Parte VII — Lo Stato Pontificio

The Papal State from the Restoration of Pius VII to the fall of the Roman Republic.

La ristorazione di Pio VII — the Restoration of Pius VII: the ecclesiastical government, the tendencies of Cardinal Consalvi, the attempted rising of Macerata, and the unquiet period. Leone XII — the papacy of Leo XII: the victory of the zelanti (zealots), the trials of Cardinal Rivarola, and the last years. Pio VIII — the brief pontificate of Pius VIII. Gregorio XVI — the pontificate of Gregory XVI: his election, the revolution of 1831, the occupation of 1832, the centurioni and the sects, the bands of 1843 and the rising of Rimini of 1845, and the reign of Gregory XVI.

Pio IX — the early pontificate of Pius IX: the amnesty, the slow steps, the reforms granted, the concessions wrested, the encyclical of 29 April, the ministry of Mamiani, the ministry of Fabbri, and the ministry of Pellegrino Rossi. La Repubblica Romana — the Roman Republic of 1849: the Constituent Assembly, the proclamation of the Republic, the government of Mazzini, the French intervention, 30 April, Velletri, the mission of Lesseps, San Pancrazio, the last defence, and the retreat of Garibaldi.

Note on the text: The OCR is Italian. The printed Indice appears near the close of the volume. Tivaroni writes as a historian of the national movement, counterposing the moderate reformism of Tuscany to the clerical absolutism of the Papal State; his treatment of the Roman Republic and its defenders is correspondingly sympathetic in tone.

Scope of the tome

Central Italy under the Austrian-restored order after 1815 and through the crises that lead into 1848–49: Tuscany, Parma, Modena, and the Papal States as Tivaroni’s “critical history” frames them—not as picturesque duchies but as police, concordat, and legation regimes inside the wider Austrian system.

Chapter spine (expanded narration)

Capitoli on the dynastic restorations. Return of Habsburg-related or Austrian-protected princes; the vacuum after Napoleonic kingdoms; how French administrative wreckage and Italian notable society meet restored courts. Tivaroni keeps a critical distance: restoration is not mere nostalgia but a security architecture backed by Austrian arms.

Police, index, and public debt. Recurring machinery—censorship, Congregazione dell’Indice, passport regimes, and the gran libro of public debt—shows how central Italian states finance themselves and discipline reading publics. These administrative chapters matter for entity pages on ministers and for concepts of restoration governance.

1848 approaches. Liberal conspiracies, moderate petitioning, and the unequal readiness of each state for statute constitutions when the revolutionary year arrives. Cross-links: Leopold II in Tuscany, Francis V of Modena, papal legations’ special harshness.

Military and diplomatic overlay. Austrian garrisons and intervention rights after earlier carbonari seasons; the sense that central Italian “independence” is conditional. Prepares the later Italia degli Italiani volumes where the same states reappear under reaction after 1849.
