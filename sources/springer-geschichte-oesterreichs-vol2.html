Springer — Geschichte Oesterreichs seit dem Wiener Frieden 1809, Vol. II: Die österreichische Revolution

Anton Springer (1825–1891) — Geschichte Oesterreichs seit dem Wiener Frieden 1809. Zweiter Theil: Die österreichische Revolution. — Leipzig: Verlag von S. Hirzel, 1865. The volume forms the companion to Springer’s Erster Theil: Der Verfall des alten Reiches (1863); together they comprise Band VI of the series Staatengeschichte der neuesten Zeit, edited by Karl Biedermann.

The second of two volumes of Springer’s history of Austria from the Treaty of Schönbrunn (1809) to the revolution of 1848. Where the first volume carried the monarchy from the Napoleonic settlement through the long policy of standstill down to the disintegration of the old Empire, the present volume — Die österreichische Revolution (The Austrian Revolution) — takes up the revolutionary year itself and carries the narrative through the risings, the constitutional experiments, and the civil war in Hungary to the restoration of absolutism.

Springer structures the volume in five books, following the revolution from its genesis to its close. He treats the movement as above all a national one: the opening chapter insists that the political struggle turned essentially on the relation of the nationalities to one another and on the measure of their state rights and privileges, and advances the maxim that “the true constitutional charter of Austria is its language map.” The narrative then moves from the growth of national consciousness and the Hungarian parties, through the March days of 1848 in Vienna, the jubilee weeks of the revolution, the Slav Congress in Prague, the parliamentary period of the Austrian and Hungarian diets, the October revolution and the Winter Campaign in Hungary, to the octroyed constitution of 1849, the summer campaign in Hungary, and the closing Schlußwort.

The volume is approximately 770 pages of text, extracted from a University of Michigan scan (Anna’s Archive).

Content Summary

Erstes Buch — Die Genesis der Revolution (The Genesis of the Revolution)

The origins of the revolutionary movement.

1. Die Entwickelung des nationalen Bewußtseins — the growth of national consciousness among the many peoples of Austria; the linguistic and ethnic map of the monarchy (the German element about 23 per cent of the population), the status of the Italian, Slavic, and Magyar nationalities, and the view that the whole political struggle turned on the relations of the nationalities to one another. 2. Die politischen Parteien in Ungarn seit 1840 — the political parties in Hungary since 1840; the reform movement, the Magyar opposition, and the growth of Hungarian political life under the old constitution. 3. Die Märztage 1848 — the March days of 1848; the outbreak of revolution in Vienna and the fall of Metternich.

Zweites Buch — Die Jubelwochen der Revolution (The Jubilee Weeks of the Revolution)

The brief triumph of the revolutionary movement in the spring of 1848.

1. Die Erbtheilung zwischen den Volksstämmen Oesterreichs — the division of the inheritance among the peoples of Austria; the competing claims of Germans, Magyars, Slavs, and Italians to a share in the new order. 2. Gründung und Sturz der Verfassung vom 25. April — the framing and overthrow of the constitution of 25 April 1848; the constitution granted by the court and its rejection in Vienna. 3. Der Slawencongreß in Prag — the Slav Congress in Prague (June 1848); the pan-Slav congress and the rising in Prague.

Drittes Buch — Die parlamentarische Periode (The Parliamentary Period)

The summer and autumn of 1848, when representative assemblies sat across the monarchy.

1. Die Landtage in den kleinen Provinzen — the diets of the smaller provinces; the provincial assemblies in the German and Slavic crownlands. 2. Der Wiener Reichstag — the Reichstag (constituent assembly) at Vienna. 3. Der Agramer Landtag — the Diet of Agram (Zagreb); the Croatian Diet and the conflict between Croatia and the Hungarian government. 4. Der ungarische Reichstag — the Hungarian Diet; the Hungarian revolutionary government under Kossuth and the quarrel with the court over Hungary’s separate constitution and the Croatian question.

Viertes Buch — Die Krisis der Revolution (The Crisis of the Revolution)

The turning of the revolution toward civil war and reaction.

1. Die Octoberrevolution — the October revolution in Vienna (October 1848); the rising of the Viennese against the government, the flight of the court, and the reconquest of the city by Windischgrätz. 2. Der Reichstag in Kremsier — the Reichstag removed to Kremsier (Kroměříž); the constituent assembly’s sittings in Moravia and its constitutional labors. 3. Der Winterfeldzug in Ungarn — the Winter Campaign in Hungary (1848–1849); the imperial military operations against the Hungarian revolutionary government.

Fünftes Buch — Die Rückkehr zum Absolutismus (The Return to Absolutism)

The restoration of imperial authority and the suppression of the revolution.

1. Die octroyirte Verfassung — the octroyed (imposed) constitution of March 1849; the dissolution of the Kremsier Reichstag and the imposition of a constitution by decree. 2. Der Sommerfeldzug in Ungarn — the Summer Campaign in Hungary (1849); the Russian intervention and the crushing of the Hungarian revolution. 3. Schlußwort — the conclusion; the final victory of reaction and the restoration of absolutism under Francis Joseph.

The volume closes with the running heading Die österreichische Revolution carrying the narrative to the re-establishment of absolutism in 1849.
