Tivaroni — Storia critica del risorgimento italiano, Vol. I: L’Italia prima della rivoluzione francese

Carlo Tivaroni (1843–1906) — Storia critica del risorgimento italiano. Volume I: L’Italia prima della rivoluzione francese. — Torino: L. Roux e C., 1888.

The first volume of Tivaroni’s critical history of the Italian Risorgimento, covering the condition of Italy in the era before the French Revolution — the decades from the settlement of the 1730s down to the eve of 1789. A positivist-minded historian, Tivaroni examines the political, social, economic, and intellectual state of the Italian states one by one, with the intention of laying bare the weaknesses and resources of the old order from which the national movement would spring. The volume is a survey of the eighteenth-century ancien régime in Italy rather than a continuous narrative.

The volume is organized in twelve Parti, each treating a state or group of states, followed by the intellectual “precursors” of the movement. It covers Venice, the Duchy of Milan, the Kingdom of Sardinia, Genoa, Modena, Parma, Tuscany, the Papal State, the Kingdom of Naples, and Sicily, and closes with essays on the adventurers, Freemasons, and rebellious thinkers of the century, and on the second half of the eighteenth century in Italy generally.

The volume is approximately 576 pages of text, extracted from a University of California scan (Anna’s Archive).

Content Summary

Parte I — La Repubblica di Venezia

Il Governo — the constitution of the republic: the Maggior Consiglio, Senate, Signoria, Doge, Council of Ten, the Inquisitors of State, the prisons of the Pozzi and Piombi, the corruption of the patriciate, and the Quarantie. Le condizioni della terraferma — the condition of the terraferma (the mainland provinces). La dominante — Venice itself: population, revenue and expenditure, patrician and clerical property, restrictions on the Church, schools and academies, literature, Goldoni and the theatre, culture and customs, the people, the Cloaca massima, the bourgeoisie, the navy and arsenal, the army, and the judgment that reform came “too late”. La vita politica — Venice’s isolation, the revolt of 1761, Angelo Quirini and Marco Foscarini, the revolt of 1775, the affair of Gratarol and the Tron family, the agitation of Contarini and Pisani (1779–80), the interrupted quiet, the relation of Tron, the Masonic lodges, and the want of any presentiment of the coming storm.

Parte II — Il Ducato di Milano

Il regime spagnuolo — the Spanish regime. Il regime austriaco di Maria Teresa — the Austrian regime of Maria Theresa: the passage from Spanish to Austrian rule, how Maria Theresa found the Lombards, and the reforms of the Austrian administration.

Parte III — Il Regno di Sardegna

Le condizioni del Piemonte — the condition of Piedmont: its foundations, the Church and education, the nobility, the Court, the army, the lower classes, taxation, the poor and the Opere pie, the budget, obstinate resistance to change, the harbingers of intellectual rebellion, and “an Italian in Piedmont”. Il regno di Carlo Emanuele III — the reign of Charles Emmanuel III. Il regno di Vittorio Amedeo III — the reign of Victor Amadeus III. La Sardegna — the island of Sardinia.

Parte IV — La Repubblica di Genova

La Costituzione e le condizioni — the constitution and condition of the republic. La ribellione del 1746 — the rebellion of 1746 against the Austrians. Genova e la Corsica — Genoa and Corsica.

Parte V — Il Ducato di Modena

Il regno di Francesco III. Il matrimonio di Beatrice d’Este — the reign of Francis III and the marriage of Beatrice d’Este. Le condizioni del Modenese — the condition of the Modenese. Ercole Rinaldo III — the reign of Hercules Rinaldo III.

Parte VI — Il Ducato di Parma

Filippo V infante di Spagna — Philip, the Spanish infant, and the establishment of the Bourbon Duchy. Guglielmo du Tillot e i regalisti — the minister du Tillot and the regalist party. Ferdinando e Maria Amalia — the reign of Ferdinand and Maria Amalia.

Parte VII — La Toscana

Il regime dei Medici — the regime of the Medici. Francesco II di Lorena — Francis of Lorraine and the passage to Austrian rule. Leopoldo I di Toscana — the great reformist reign of Grand Duke Leopold. Il vescovo Scipione de Ricci — the reforming bishop Scipione de’ Ricci.

Potenza degli Ecclesiastici — the power of the clergy. Gli ultimi atti di Leopoldo — the last acts of Leopold’s reign. La Repubblica di Lucca — the Republic of Lucca.

Parte VIII — Gli Stati della Chiesa

Il doppio potere — the double (spiritual and temporal) power. Le finanze pontificie — the pontifical finances. Il governo dei preti — priestly government: property, display, administration, the administration of justice, the Inquisition and education, and clerics in office. I principi romani — the Roman princes.

Roma caput mundi — Rome as head of the world. Papi inconcludenti — the inconclusive popes. Clemente XIV e la soppressione dei Gesuiti — Clement XIV and the suppression of the Jesuits (1773). Pio VI — the pontificate of Pius VI.

Parte IX — Il Regno di Napoli

Spagnuoli ed Austriaci — Spaniards and Austrians. Il Clero — the clergy. I baroni — the barons and the feudal system. Il popolo e la terra — the people and the land.

L’organismo — the political organism. Legislazione economica — economic legislation. L’istruzione pubblica — public education. Il movimento intellettuale — the intellectual movement: Antonio Genovesi, G.M. Galanti, Gaetano Filangieri.

Carlo III — the reign of Charles III. Il regno di Ferdinando IV — the reign of Ferdinand IV: Bernardo Tanucci, King Ferdinand, the policy of the Queen, Maria Carolina.

Parte X — La Sicilia

L’antica Costituzione — the ancient constitution of Sicily. I baroni e le condizioni sociali — the barons and social conditions. Le finanze — the finances. Il Clero — the clergy.

Il Governo dei Borboni di Napoli in Sicilia dal 1735 al 1780 — the government of the Neapolitan Bourbons in Sicily from 1735 to 1780. Il Vicerè Caracciolo — the reforming viceroy Domenico Caracciolo.

Parte XI — I Precursori

Tre avventurieri del secolo XVIII — three adventurers of the eighteenth century: Casanova, Cagliostro, and Count Giuseppe Gorani. La Massoneria — Freemasonry. I pensatori ribelli — the rebellious thinkers: Radicati di Passerano, Carlo Antonio Pilati, and other lesser figures. Parini — the poet Giuseppe Parini.

Vittorio Alfieri — the tragedian Vittorio Alfieri and the passion for liberty. Mario Pagano — the jurist Francesco Mario Pagano.

Parte XII — La seconda metà del secolo XVIII in Italia

The closing survey of the second half of the eighteenth century in Italy.

Note on the text: The OCR is Italian. The printed Indice appears near the close of the volume. Tivaroni writes as a historian of the national movement, so the survey of the ancien régime is oriented toward explaining the eventual collapse of the old order and the rise of the Risorgimento.
