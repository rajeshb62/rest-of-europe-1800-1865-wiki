Springer — Geschichte Oesterreichs seit dem Wiener Frieden 1809, Vol. I: Der Verfall des alten Reiches

Anton Springer (1825–1891) — Geschichte Oesterreichs seit dem Wiener Frieden 1809. Erster Theil: Der Verfall des alten Reiches. — Leipzig: Verlag von S. Hirzel, 1863. The volume forms Band VI of the series Staatengeschichte der neuesten Zeit, edited by Karl Biedermann.

The first of two volumes of Springer’s history of Austria from the Treaty of Schönbrunn (1809) to the revolution of 1848. The present volume — Der Verfall des alten Reiches (The Decline of the Old Empire) — carries the monarchy from the condition it had reached at the end of the Napoleonic wars, through the long policy of standstill under Emperor Francis I and Prince Metternich, the financial crises and the Hungarian Diets, down to the disintegration of the governing powers and the collapse of the old Empire on the eve of the revolutionary year 1848. Its companion volume, Die österreichische Revolution (1865), continues the narrative through the revolution itself.

Springer, a historian of art and of the German national movement, writes in the German-Fraktur historical idiom of the era, treating Austria as a European great power whose internal structure — the estates, the crownlands, the Hungarian constitution, and the claims of the non-German nationalities — was dissolving even as its diplomatic standing held. The volume is notable for its extended treatment of the Hungarian Diets and of the constitutional friction between Vienna and Pesth (Budapest).

The text is approximately 580 pages, extracted from a New York Public Library scan (Anna’s Archive).

Content Summary

Erstes Buch — Die gegebenen Zustände (The Existing Conditions)

An introductory book describing the state of the Austrian monarchy at the close of the Napoleonic era and the foundations on which its subsequent policy rested.

1. Einleitung — an introduction affirming Austria’s political necessity and its role in the European balance of power. 2. Die Reaction gegen Josephs II. Reformen — the reaction against the reforming spirit of Joseph II; the conservative restoration that followed the revolutionary and Napoleonic wars. 3. Die Kriegsjahre 1792 bis 1809 — the war years from 1792 to the Treaty of Schönbrunn (1809); Austria’s struggles against Revolutionary and Napoleonic France.

Zweites Buch — Die Begründung der Politik des Stillstandes und Gleichgewichtes (The Founding of the Policy of Standstill and Balance)

The establishment under Francis I and Metternich of a system of political stagnation and equilibrium.

1. Kaiser Franz und Metternich — the character and policy of Emperor Francis I and his minister Metternich; the principles of the system of standstill. 2. Das Finanzpatent — the Financial Patent (the great state bankruptcy of 1811) and the handling of the Austrian finances. 3. Der ungarische Reichstag 1811 — the Hungarian Diet of 1811 and the friction between the crown and the Hungarian estates. 4. Die Befreiungskriege — the Wars of Liberation (1813–1815); Austria’s part in the overthrow of Napoleon and the settlement of the Congress of Vienna.

Drittes Buch — Die Jubeljahre der Reaction (The Jubilee Years of Reaction)

The high point of the conservative restoration after 1815.

1. Die Wiederherstellung der österreichischen Macht — the restoration of Austrian power in the European system after the fall of Napoleon. 2. Der Sieg über den Liberalismus — the victory over liberalism; the suppression of liberal and national aspirations within the monarchy. 3. Die Regelung der Finanzen — the regulation of the finances after the disorders of the war years.

Viertes Buch — Schwankungen und Rückschritte (Vacillations and Setbacks)

The growing strain on the system of standstill in the later years of Francis I.

1. Der ungarische Reichstag 1825 — the Hungarian Diet of 1825; the renewed constitutional conflict with Hungary. 2. Die orientalische Frage — the Eastern Question; Austria’s involvement in the affairs of the Ottoman Empire. 3. Die letzten Lebensjahre des Kaiser Franz — the last years of Emperor Francis (d. 1835); the end of the old emperor’s reign.

Fünftes Buch — Die Zersetzung der Regierungsgewalten (The Disintegration of the Governing Powers)

The decline of the old Empire on the eve of 1848.

1. Die Vormundschaft der Staatsconferenz — the guardianship of the State Conference; the regency-like council that governed in the name of the feeble Emperor Ferdinand I (1835–1848). 2. Der Operatenreichstag — the long Hungarian Diet of 1832–1836, so called for the royal reform propositions (Operate) laid before it; the language and nationality questions, the railway and taxation debates, the closing of the Diet in 1836. 3. Die ständische Bewegung in den deutsch-slawischen Ländern — the estates movement in the German and Slavic crownlands (including Bohemia); the stirring of national sentiment among the subject peoples. 4. Der Verfall des alten Reiches — the decline and fall of the old Empire; the disintegration of the governing powers that opened the way to the revolution of 1848.

The volume closes with the running heading Der Verfall des alten Reiches carrying the narrative to the threshold of the revolutionary year.

Note on the text: The OCR is German Fraktur with the original long-s (ſ), which renders correctly in the raw text. Running headers repeat identically on facing pages and carry the book titles. The full table of contents (Inhalt) appears at the opening of the volume. The Operate in the chapter Der Operatenreichstag is the Hungarian administrative term for the royal reform propositions submitted to the Diet.
