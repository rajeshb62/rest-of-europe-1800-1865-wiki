# Friedrich List — Das nationale System der Politischen Ökonomie

**Friedrich List (1789–1846)** — *Das nationale System der politischen Oekonomie.* — Erster Band: Der internationale Handel, die Handelspolitik und die deutsche Zollunion. — Stuttgart und Tübingen: Verlag der J. G. Cotta'schen Buchhandlung, 1841.

Foundational text of the German historical school of economics and one of the most influential works of 19th-century economic thought. List develops a systematic critique of Adam Smith's classical political economy, arguing that free trade benefits only industrially advanced nations while disadvantaging developing economies. He advocates for economic nationalism: protective tariffs for infant industries, state-led industrialization, and the creation of a German national market through the Zollverein (customs union).

The work is essential for understanding the economic philosophy that shaped German unification policy and the intellectual foundations of the Zollverein. List's ideas — first developed in his 1818 petition to the Federal Assembly and elaborated through his exile in America and his work as a railway promoter in Germany — directly influenced Bismarck's later economic policies and the German Empire's protectionist turn in 1879.

Approximately 600 pages. From a Bayerische Staatsbibliothek (BSB) scan.

## Content Summary

The work is divided into three main sections, following List's intellectual journey from his earliest economic writings to the mature synthesis of 1841.

### Einleitung: Die Geschichte der Nationalökonomie (Introduction: The History of Political Economy)

A critical survey of economic thought from the mercantilists through the physiocrats to Adam Smith and Jean-Baptiste Say. List argues that Smith's system, while appropriate for England — already the world's leading industrial power — cannot serve as a universal model. The "cosmopolitan" or "universal" economy of Smith ignores the political realities of national development.

- Critique of Smith's "cosmopolitical" system
- The physiocratic school and its limitations
- The Italian economists (Beccaria, Verri, etc.)
- The mercantilist system reconsidered
- The role of national interest in economic policy

### Buch I: Die Theorie (The Theory)

List's positive exposition of his national system of political economy. He develops the concept of "productive powers" (Produktivkräfte) as distinct from exchange value, arguing that a nation's true wealth lies in its capacity to produce, not merely in accumulated material wealth.

- **1. Kapitel: Die Produktivkräfte und die Werttheorie** — Productive forces vs. the theory of values; the importance of mental and social capital
- **2. Kapitel: Die Arbeitsteilung und die Kräftevereinigung** — The division of labor and the confederation of productive forces; how national unity enhances productivity
- **3. Kapitel: Die materielle Produktion und die immaterielle Produktion** — Material and immaterial production; the role of education, law, and state institutions in economic development
- **4. Kapitel: Die Landwirtschaft, die Industrie und der Handel** — Agriculture, industry, and commerce as interdependent sectors; the primacy of industrial development
- **5. Kapitel: Die Industrie als Grundlage der Nationalwirtschaft** — Industry as the foundation of a national economy; the multiplier effects of manufacturing
- **6. Kapitel: Die Erziehungszölle** — Educational tariffs (Erziehungszölle) as a tool for protecting infant industries until they can compete internationally
- **7. Kapitel: Die Handelspolitik der Nationen** — The commercial policy of nations; stages of economic development (pastoral, agricultural, agricultural-manufacturing, agricultural-manufacturing-commercial)

### Buch II: Die Systeme der Handelspolitik (The Systems of Commercial Policy)

A historical examination of the trade policies of the major powers, designed to show that England achieved its industrial supremacy through protectionism, then preached free trade to prevent others from following the same path.

- **England** — The English navigation acts, the protective system of Cromwell and the Stuarts; England's rise to industrial dominance behind tariff barriers
- **Frankreich** — Colbertism and French industrial policy; the Napoleonic Continental System
- **Deutschland** — German economic fragmentation; the internal tariffs and trade barriers between German states
- **Nordamerika** — Alexander Hamilton's report on manufactures; the American protective system; List's own experience in the United States
- **The Theory of Free Trade as an English Strategy** — List's argument that free trade doctrine serves English interests

### Buch III: Die Politik und die Nationalwirtschaft (Politics and the National Economy)

The practical application of List's theory to German conditions. This is the most historically important section, as it directly advocates for the German Zollverein and national unity.

- **Der Deutsche Zollverein** — The history and achievements of the German Customs Union; its role as a precursor to political unification
- **Die Eisenbahnen und das nationale Transportsystem** — Railways as a tool for national integration; List's own role in promoting German railway construction
- **Die maritime Stellung Deutschlands** — Germany's maritime position; the need for a German navy and merchant marine
- **Die deutsche Industrie und der Welthandel** — German industry and world trade; the competitive position of German manufactures
- **Das nationale System als Grundlage der Zollpolitik** — The national system as the foundation of tariff policy; practical recommendations for Zollverein tariff rates

## Notes

List first formulated his economic ideas in his 1818 petition to the Federal Assembly (reproduced as an appendix in some editions). The work went through multiple editions after List's death in 1846 and was translated into English, French, Russian, and Japanese. The standard English edition is *The National System of Political Economy*, translated by Sampson S. Lloyd (London: Longmans, Green, and Co., 1885).
