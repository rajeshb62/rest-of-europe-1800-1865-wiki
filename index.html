Rest of Europe 1800–1865

A narrative-research knowledge base on continental European history from the Napoleonic settlement through 1865 (eve of the Austro-Prussian War). The United Kingdom is a separate wiki. Pages are grounded in the deposited source corpus — German national narrative sources (Treitschke, Sybel, List), Austrian revolutionary and absolutist sources (Springer, Friedjung, Helfert), Italian Risorgimento (La Farina, Tivaroni), French parliamentary and imperial history (Duvergier, Delord, Ollivier), Hungarian reform (Grünwald, Springer), and Russian nineteenth-century lectures (Kornilov).

Narrative arc

Napoleonic Italy and the Congress of Vienna reset the peninsula and the Germanies under Austrian presidency while Prussia prepared the commercial path of the Zollverein (1834). The Concert of Europe and Holy Alliance framed legitimacy; Troppau–Laibach–Verona turned congress diplomacy into intervention after 1820. Alexander I’s Russia mixed reform openings and alliance politics; Decembrists, then Nicholas I and Official Nationality (Uvarov), closed the door. Restoration France lived under the Charte and the Doctrinaires; the July Monarchy of Louis-Philippe, Guizot, and Thiers ran the pays légal until February 1848. Carlsbad, Burschenschaften, and Hambach marked the German Vormärz; List’s national system theorized protection behind customs unity. The Rhine crisis of 1840 tested Orléanist foreign policy and German national feeling together.

1848–49 broke the Metternich system: Paris in February and the June Days (National Workshops, Cavaignac); Vienna’s March and October; Milan’s Five Days; Hungary’s April Laws, Batthyány, Széchenyi’s reform legacy, war of independence, Jelačić and Windischgrätz, Görgei and Világos; Frankfurt’s failed constitution; Rome’s republic and the death of neo-Guelphism; Novara, Charles Albert’s abdication, and d’Azeglio’s Piedmont under the Statuto; Russian intervention in Hungary. Schwarzenberg and the young Francis Joseph rebuilt power; Radowitz’s Erfurt Union failed at Olmütz (Manteuffel); the Silvesterpatent, Kübeck, and Bach defined absolutism after 1848, sealed spiritually by the Concordat of 1855.

The 1850s–60s reopened national questions. The Crimean War isolated Russia and seated Sardinia at Paris; Orsini and Plombières led to Magenta and Solferino, Villafranca, the Thousand, Gaeta, and the Kingdom of Italy (1861) under Cavour, Victor Emmanuel, Garibaldi, then Ricasoli, Farini, and Rattazzi — Roman question still open, Ferdinand II’s Naples gone. Austria’s October Diploma and February Patent began constitutional retreat from Bach’s system. Bismarck’s conflict ministry under Wilhelm I and Roon, the Schleswig-Holstein war of 1864, and Gastein reopened dualism (kleindeutsch / großdeutsch). Alexander II’s Emancipation Manifesto (1861) opened the Great Reforms. France’s Second Empire moved from coup authoritarian core (Morny, Persigny) toward talk of a liberal Empire.

How to read

  1. Start with major events (Vienna 1815; congress intervention; 1830; 1848 clusters; Crimea; 1859–61 Italy; Austrian patents; 1864 Denmark).
  2. Read entities as continuous political biographies tied to those crises.
  3. Use concepts for structures that span decades (Metternich system, Zollverein, Bach system, Charte, Risorgimento, nationality question, Official Nationality, Napoleonic Italy).
  4. Every entity/event/concept page ends with a Sources section pointing at the underlying books.
